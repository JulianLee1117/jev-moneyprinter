# Tenaris materiality review — September 18, 2026

**Recommendation: retain OCTG–TS as an extraction/control case, but do not activate its price pilot yet.** The direct cash mechanism is real; a material *new* cash increment remains unquantified. Broader issuer-first discovery is a better use of the next research budget than more questions about the same TAMSA notice. This review uses no price series, paid inference or orders.

## What the additional evidence changes

The August 5-dated half-year report (SEC-filed August 7) explicitly repeats the Mexican **1.62% preliminary rate** and expects a final result in September–November. It also reports that Argentine first-review refunds were collected in March 2026. This is already issuer-distributed information. The separate mechanical-pipe scope issue has a **$49.6m** CBP assessment for 2022–24, no recorded provision, a favorable July 16 preliminary ruling, and an expected December final. Since January 2025, separate deposits continue. Neither that historical assessment nor its removal equals a $49.6m new refund or earnings gain. [SEC half-year filing, Note 18](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1190723/000117184326005340/f6k_080526.htm)

The mechanical-pipe inquiry is excluded from the registered OCTG–TS universe. It could be a separately registered development example, but its post-2025 deposit total is also missing. A favorable preliminary finding already exists, and unrecognized contingent liabilities do not create a provision reversal when dismissed. Do not add its asserted $49.6m to an OCTG refund estimate: exact entries, periods and legal coverage must first be reconciled.

Tenaris reported FY2025 EBITDA of **$2,899.386m**, including a **$34m** Argentine antidumping gain. The gain is only **1.17%** of that annual EBITDA and is a one-time historical recognition, not annual recurring income or evidence of what a trader could have predicted in June 2025. The company reported 2025 free cash flow of approximately $2.0bn. [FY2025 results](https://ir.tenaris.com/news-releases/news-release-details/tenaris-announces-2025-fourth-quarter-and-annual-results)

[Q1 results](https://ir.tenaris.com/news-releases/news-release-details/tenaris-announces-2026-first-quarter-results), [Q2 results](https://ir.tenaris.com/news-releases/news-release-details/tenaris-announces-2026-second-quarter-results)

## The denominator is still the bottleneck

Reviewed primary material did **not** provide TAMSA's covered U.S. entered value for November 2023–October 2024, outstanding deposits for that period, a company importer-specific assessment rate, or annual future eligible import value. This is qualified search evidence, not a claim that no public docket ever contains the numbers. The original USITC factual highlights explicitly withhold subject import totals to avoid proprietary disclosure; even disclosed national totals would not by themselves establish the company's covered denominator. [USITC factual highlights](https://www.usitc.gov/keywords/oil_country_tubular_goods)

The preliminary notice requires importer-specific assessment calculations based on examined sales and entered values, with an alternative per-unit method where needed. Thus the published exporter margin is not a safe substitute for the company's retrospective refund assessment. [Official May 13 notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-05-13/html/2026-09464.htm)

Use three separate calculations:

1. **Forward gross deposit cash:** if a final 1.62% replaces the currently cited 26.10%, the mechanical reduction is 24.48 percentage points times future *covered entered value*. Every hypothetical $100m of that annual value implies $24.48m less gross annual deposit outflow, before volume changes, offsets, tax and final assessment differences. This is a sensitivity, not a discovered revenue estimate or EBITDA forecast.
2. **Retrospective refund:** deposits on the November 2023–October 2024 review-period entries were under the older 44.93% schedule, subject to exact entry instructions. They did not originate at September 2025's 26.10% schedule. A purely illustrative 44.93% minus 1.62% is 43.31 points, but actual refunds require deposit records, importer-specific final assessment and lawful liquidation. They cannot be calculated from that margin subtraction alone.
3. **New final information:** the relevant public proposal is already 1.62%. A final matching it has zero *rate-number difference from that proposal*, while it may change legal certainty, timing and the probability of implementation. Those resolution effects are not zero by definition, but the full 24.48-point operative change is not automatically a fresh market surprise. Every one-point unexpected rate change produces $1m gross annual cash sensitivity per hypothetical $100m of covered annual value.

For scale only, a fully realized 24.48-point forward reduction would require approximately **$118.44m** of annual covered value to equal 1% of FY2025 EBITDA, or **$592.19m** to equal 5%. These are researcher-chosen illustrative levels, **not economic gate thresholds, import estimates, equity-return forecasts or optimized cutoffs**. Comparing cash deposits with EBITDA is a scale comparison; their recognition and ultimate cost differ.

## Effective next step

Keep the existing price gate closed. Reopen only on company-specific deposit/entry data or a defensible, as-of public bound; a genuinely new legal increment; and timing/offset evidence sufficient for the claimed effect. Searches should first look for public importer assessment instructions, company deposit balances by review period, or quantified issuer sensitivity. Stop escalating document/model volume if the underlying dollars are confidential.

For a broader Jev experiment, reverse the join: find issuers that already disclose named trade-remedy deposits or quantified contingencies, then map their subsidiaries, vendors, exact cases, periods and legal changes. Jev's useful role is many cheap relevance, exception and contradiction judgments across this larger graph, while arithmetic and dates remain deterministic. Selection must be frozen before prices and include null/procedural cases. A cheaper classifier on one long-known TAMSA proposal is not in itself a tradable advantage.

This review does not reject the possibility of a Tenaris opportunity. It rejects treating an unmeasured, already-discussed rate proposal as an established alpha candidate.
