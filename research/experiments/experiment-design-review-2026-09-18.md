# Independent experiment-design review

Review date: 2026-09-18, America/Los_Angeles. Scope: the repository's recorded CORE and OCTG experiments, not a new financial recommendation. No prices, return series, paid model calls, or orders were requested by this reviewer. This document proposes work; it is not a claim that the proposed run has occurred or that its labels have been frozen.

## Decision

**Run the inexpensive date-only return diagnostic now, alongside one frozen test of Jev's evidence-coverage advantage. Do not make either test wait for every trading-readiness field.** The current system has useful engineering and realistic legal distinctions, but almost all empirical effort has measured model interpretation. The money question still has no outcome evidence.

The strongest economic candidate in this repository is the direct importer cash-flow mechanism for Tenaris. That is a causal attribution advantage, not an established market edge. Three registered case episodes and one possible future final announcement cannot support a repeatable-strategy conclusion. Waiting until November for one prospective outcome would be an inefficient use of the available compute and research time.

The scarce resource is presently evidence of useful information and a sufficiently frequent opportunity set. More model calls and more guardrails do not create either. After the bounded diagnostic, expand the *discovery census* across public trade-remedy cases with verified listed-company links, while keeping each new hypothesis and any outcome inspection in an experiment registry. Do not expand a profitable-looking historical subset after seeing its returns and call it a holdout.

## Which gates belong where

The current plan requires unknown economic magnitude to block price-pilot readiness, three fully resolved episodes, and resolution or exclusion of every selected dossier. The OCTG protocol also requires importer-specific denominators and auditable intraday availability before an executable paper observation. Those are defensible requirements for their stated strong claims. They are unnecessarily strict if interpreted as preventing any free daily diagnostic.

- Exact intraday first-public time is essential for a latency or executable-entry claim. It is not required to measure what happened *after an explicitly dated, conservatively delayed daily observation*. Earlier disclosure remains an explicit confound. A late publication-date result cannot establish the value of being first.
- Missing issuer-specific import value prevents a defensible cash-flow estimate. It need not prevent recording whether an already selected, directionally hypothesized case was followed by any residual daily movement. Keep unknown magnitude visible and do not remove those cases after inspecting returns.
- An unknown market expectation prevents calling final-minus-preliminary an expectations surprise. It does not prevent testing a predeclared rate/state proxy. Label the proxy honestly.
- One or two cases cannot estimate reliable alpha. They can expose a completely unpromising economic scale, a source that arrives too late, broken market-data handling, or a signal whose apparent result comes entirely from broad market moves. These findings are useful before a large build.
- Requiring all twenty CORE dossiers to be fully resolved blocks unrelated informative cases. Freeze an explicit bounded diagnostic cohort and retain incomplete cases in its denominator, instead of silently making completeness an outcome-dependent selector.

Create a separate registered daily diagnostic instead of weakening the existing intraday or live-trade protocol. Its return rule, side/abstention policy, source date, event grouping, costs/sensitivity presentation, benchmark and missing-data handling must be saved before outcome requests. If the root experiment uses several horizons, identify one primary horizon before looking and report every secondary result. Do not select the best horizon afterward.

## What the existing model numbers establish

The 23/24 versus 21/24 transition result comprises two manually selected cases. Its questions share the same documents; they are not 24 independent discoveries. Both models received manually selected passages and transcribed tables. This evaluates interpretation after substantial research assistance. Many answers concern absent evidence, which is useful but not the scarce profitable task. The same mixed-direction rate example defeated both models, and arithmetic now catches it.

The prior passage run retained 167 of 195 passages. It demonstrates broad evaluation, but removing only about 14% of passages is not yet an economically persuasive reduction in review effort. The two-label coverage comparison is a development diagnostic, not comprehensive recall. Report full-source reading as a ceiling and a real alternative when the source is short enough.

The reported 14–17-fold API savings are real observations against one selected conventional baseline. Dollar savings of cents on a handful of documents are not a moat. A baseline forced to emit every probability can also incur avoidable output cost: compare the actual useful workflow at matched review cost, not only a schema made congenial to Jev. Jev should win because its broader judgments produce additional correct, timely opportunities or materially reduce the cost of finding them.

TypeSafe's primary documentation supports parallel questions, predefined option choices and full distributions. Its own launch methodology uses other models' probabilities as reference on workflow tasks; that is not independent financial ground truth. The project must separately test domain calibration and semantic accuracy. Schema validity does not prevent a semantically wrong choice, as this repository's live cases already show. ## One concrete frozen experiment available now

**Experiment proposal: CORE-20-consequential-evidence-coverage-v1.** This is an engineering test of whether Jev can find more relevant facts per unit of review. It is not an alpha test and must run alongside, rather than replace, the return diagnostic.

1. **Corpus:** use every one of the twenty already archived full-source packets named in `core-pilot-v2.json`. Freeze source byte hashes and the source-state manifest before producing labels or new retrieval outputs. Preserve all twenty, including procedural notices. Known reviewed cases remain development cases; no part of this corpus is advertised as a pristine holdout.
2. **Consequential-fact labels:** reviewers read complete documents without seeing the selector outputs. Enumerate minimal source spans establishing a rate, applicability limitation, operative/proposed status, deposit-versus-assessment treatment, timing trigger, conflicting statement, rescission/exclusion, or explicit absence of a new change. Every label must have exact source spans and a rationale. A second independently prompted reviewer adjudicates a deterministic sample and all disagreements; unresolved labels remain unresolved. Calling these agent-adjudicated labels is essential. Do not use Jev's retained passages to generate its own answer key.
3. **Three selectors:** existing deterministic keyword/neighborhood retrieval; frozen Jev passage classifications and probabilities; one conventional-model passage selector on the same source chunks and evidence. Include the full source as a recall ceiling with its complete review-text cost. Use current code-defined numeric/date arithmetic in downstream checks for all arms.
4. **Primary review budget:** 50% of each document's Unicode source characters, including context and exception spans. This is a predeclared engineering target, not an empirically justified safety threshold. Rank passages by each method's frozen relevance rule, include whole passages until the budget would be exceeded, and document the deterministic tie rule. Failures or oversized necessary passages must be visible, not dropped. Existing unconstrained high-recall selection is a separately reported diagnostic, not the primary arm. Do not choose the budget after looking at which labels were retained.
5. **Primary metric:** complete consequential-fact support recall, first per document and then averaged across the twenty documents. A fact requiring several spans is retained only when all are present. Report exception/conflict recall separately, as well as the count of documents retaining every resolved critical fact. Do not let many easy fragments in a long document dominate the answer.
6. **Secondary metrics:** retained characters, total input/output tokens and dollars, end-to-end elapsed time, missing/invalid calls, review and adjudication minutes if actually measured, and critical facts recovered only by Jev. Cache replay costs and original inference costs remain distinct. A reviewer seeing full text is an actual alternative to quantify, not a zero-cost fictional baseline.
7. **Budget and termination:** prepare every request first and run only if the existing project budget ledger allows it. A maximum $5 incremental API reservation for this bounded test is adequate as a proposed ceiling; actual preflight estimates take precedence. No automatic retry after uncertain outcomes. No prompt/threshold revision within the primary run. Revisions receive a new experiment ID and retain the failed result.

The current source archive, fanout planner, coverage module, finite-cost ledger, and comparison transport already provide most of the machinery. Labeling, ranked budget selection, and the conventional selector are the bounded additions. The financial utility is narrower than profitability: this tests whether wide Jev screening reduces the costly human evidence-review bottleneck without hiding exceptions. It does not tell us whether the preserved facts move prices.

## Practical stop and continue rules

- **Retrieval-route stop:** if Jev does not recover additional critical evidence versus deterministic retrieval at the same review volume, and does not reduce total useful-work cost versus a conventional selector, do not build a more elaborate Jev retrieval stack for this corpus. Keep the cheaper adequate method. A tie on these twenty documents is inconclusive about all domains, but sufficient to stop expanding this particular implementation.
- **Filtering stop:** if a useful recall level requires retaining almost the entire source, use broad reading/chunking and reserve Jev for entity/applicability/state comparisons. Do not call near-total retention an efficiency win. Any missed operative exception requires review before automatic exclusion, even if average recall looks high.
- **Economic scale stop:** if a defensible upper bound is immaterial to the issuer under the prespecified research target, reject that event thesis. Avoid pretending a public industry-wide import denominator is a company-specific estimate.
- **Frequency stop:** a company-family with one forthcoming observation is a watchlist case, not a complete strategy. Record the sparse census and extend discovery to other directly exposed public issuers under a new pre-outcome design; do not spend months calibrating 28 labels against one event.
- **Timing stop:** repeated cases where the actionable information precedes the monitored source reject the Federal Register-only alert channel. Move to the demonstrated earlier public source if it can be collected, while keeping the failed channel result.
- **Financial diagnostic interpretation:** unfavorable tiny-sample returns do not statistically disprove the mechanism; favorable ones do not prove it. A result that disappears under the frozen delayed-entry rule, broad-market adjustment or declared cost sensitivity does reject the claimed *observed* opportunity in that sample. Do not rescue it with a shorter hindsight-selected entry.
- **Before capital:** a positive historical diagnostic earns a larger independently grouped test and prospective collection. It does not turn interpretation probabilities into win probabilities or authorize an order.

## Recommended allocation of the next effort

Complete the daily diagnostic and the economic-scale work first. Spend a bounded minority of the next increment on the CORE-20 coverage test. If the direct TS mechanism is small or too sparse, the next substantial compute expenditure should be broader source-to-listed-company discovery with frozen economics and dates, not repeated prompt tuning on the two known transition examples. Preserve every branch's cost, rejected cases and outcome access so the final account reflects the entire search.
