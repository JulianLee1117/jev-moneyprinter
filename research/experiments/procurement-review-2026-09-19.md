# Procurement discovery experiment — September 19, 2026

The bounded source review found **zero independently verified eligible projects**, below the registered minimum of 30 projects across four issuers. The decision is to stop expansion of this source roster. The evidence does not establish procurement alpha or a profitable Jev timing advantage. Coverage remains incomplete, so it also does not establish that all public procurement information lacks value.

The [protocol](procurement-protocol.v1.json) fixed six contractors, ten buyer sources, July–December 2025, a chronological October–December holdout, a $1,000 hypothetical account and a $50 combined model cap. The [runbook](procurement-runbook.md) documents the implemented command sequence. The bounded census, model comparison, available quote diagnostic and independent contribution assessment are complete. The machine-readable result is [procurement-results-2026-09-19.json](procurement-results-2026-09-19.json).

## What the census covered

The collector catalogued 5,771 unique document records, including navigation inventories and failed captures, with 5,501 unique raw hashes. Preparation retained 5,085 documents and 64,015 diagnostic packets representing 293,561 source-linked targets. These are not counts of contracts or independent events. Only 50 packets from eight documents required model inference: 37 development and 13 holdout, from Port Tampa Bay and TxDOT. Shared deterministic gates accounted for the remaining packets; unknown extraction, currency and ownership stayed unknown.

The ten-source counts were Broward 1,000; Santa Clara Valley Water District 629; Los Angeles Metro 412; San Antonio zero; King County 1,000; Seattle 1,000; Port Tampa Bay 310; Port Seattle 856; TxDOT 167; and FDOT 397. The 10,000-record maximum was allocated at 1,000 per source, with package order fixed by hash before model results or returns. Three sources reached that allocation; the global ceiling was not reached. No failed source was replaced.

Every source remains marked incomplete. San Antonio returned no events; some meeting packages lacked agendas or minutes; the HTML adapters did not establish exhaustive enumeration. The title screen inventoried 5,839 exclusions without downloading all their attachments. Its independent 50-title audit found four unresolved cases, and cannot establish an attachment-level omission rate. There were 150 failed captures, including 404/403 responses, byte-limit failures and connection resets. Preparation recorded 686 exclusions: 674 inventory/extraction-unresolved records, four earlier meeting documents and eight genuinely later reports.

The initial source-balanced, hash-selected 100-document feasibility review came from its recorded earlier collection checkpoint. Final independent agent source reviews covered every model-required packet and 90 source-stratified sampled rejects: **140 packets from 89 documents, 133 ineligible and seven unresolved**. Reviewers read archived sources and checked selected PDF layouts without model outputs or prices. The unresolved rows represent five source-local project identities. Grouped exempt targets were reviewed at their representative-target level. Neither the initial feasibility sample nor final purposive review proves full-corpus recall.

## Source meaning and time mattered more than inference throughput

Reviewers found current-status tables mistaken for new awards, engineer estimates mistaken for contractor value, nonmonetary quantities parsed as money, scanned/missing exhibits and incomplete ownership evidence. A $427 fee followed by section B produced a false billion-scale candidate; source review preserved that extraction defect instead of treating it as a real award. A genuine $107.409 million Primoris-related award was below the fixed 2% revenue threshold; the larger engineer estimate was not contractor revenue.

Date checks also found old minutes attached to later meetings and updated reports carrying earlier event associations. Corrections and exclusions occurred before paid inference: four documents were moved outside the period; eight 2026 reports were excluded; later in-period report dates were retained; and FDOT bid-report dates remained distinct from letting/award dates. Thirty-two residual header/package differences are documented as coverage limitations. No trustworthy historical first-publication clock was established. Present-day downloads and printed meeting dates cannot support an executable historical-profit or arrival-speed claim.

Seven dated annual-revenue observations and 57 verified aliases supported deterministic attribution. The alias map is explicitly partial. Unknown ownership, JV work shares, values and prior project histories were not filled by model confidence.

## Model comparison

Jev used seven native parallel questions and retained its distributions. Nano and mini returned efficient structured categories, with reasoning explicitly disabled. All arms used identical evidence, amount candidates, questions and exemptions. The registered trading rule used categorical decisions and deterministic materiality arithmetic; confidence, funding conditions and proof of surprise were not added as gates.

Jev and nano each completed all 50 required calls. Mini completed 41, with nine development HTTP 429 attempts remaining unresolved and never resubmitted. All three completed the 13 holdout calls. Development selected eight workers for Jev/nano and one for mini; the latter's higher-concurrency groups were incomplete. Cached answers supplied no fresh latency observations.

On identical development allocations, Jev's fresh batch throughput was about 2.9–3.4 times nano's. The fresh 13-packet holdout execution phase took 0.929 seconds for Jev, 3.375 seconds for nano and 17.657 seconds for mini. Complete run times were 44.131, 60.653 and 75.768 seconds respectively; request preparation accounted for much of that time. These are small offline batches, not actual market arrivals. Cross-concurrency groups contained different packets. Faster API processing does not establish an earlier achievable entry.

Across 350 independently labeled categories in the 50 required packets, matches were Jev 212, nano 207, mini 181 and rules 166. Mini's denominator includes 63 unanswered categories. The arms matched every category in two, zero, three and zero packets respectively. These source-category diagnostics are not trading-success rates; zero true eligible projects makes useful-event recall unmeasurable.

A final source-identity audit reconciled seven reviewer IDs across four Port Tampa contracts before signals or prices were frozen. Prefix variants such as `24-43` and `PTB-24-43` had described the same contract. The revised review changes only project IDs, with source-backed provenance; all source categories and model answers remain unchanged. Earlier review/quality artifacts are retained as superseded versions.

Jev's four positive packets all described one old Orion Berth 218 contract, using repeated approved balances or incurred costs as if they were new work. Nano's five positive packets represented four canonical projects: an old Orion cost balance, two Russell Marine observations wrongly attributed to Orion, a Liebherr crane contract wrongly attributed to Orion, and a Primoris engineer estimate substituted for the smaller actual award. The source audit established these errors before prices. The financial diagnostic retains such model mistakes rather than replacing them with correct gold judgments. Any coincidental stock gain on an ineligible document would not demonstrate the registered award mechanism.

## Frozen decisions and available returns

After deduplication, the frozen rules arm proposed no projects; Jev proposed one, mini two and nano four. All proposed projects were independently judged ineligible. Gold labels did not cancel or repair their model-generated signals.

Read-only market collection captured 57 requests covering the exchange calendar, seven daily-bar series, seven corporate-action responses and 42 quote windows. Thirty-five quote windows completed, six IWM windows retained truncated first pages, and ROAD's September 17 window was empty. Retained first-page quotes can support a first-qualifying-quote selection where present; truncation does not justify assuming later missing quotes. Independently captured issuer/fund evidence resolved all 21 stock/benchmark action intervals, with no owned dividend or unsupported holder action. Later annual filings served only as retrospective adjustment corroboration, not model input.

**Nano's single holdout trade lost $18.07 after trading and holdout inference costs on a $1,000 starting account**, or $20.84 under the 25-basis-point stress. It bought eight PRIM shares using the first sufficient ask of $116.41 on November 14 and exited at the first sufficient bid of $114.51 on November 21. Base trading P&L was −$18.04736 before $0.01948565 of holdout inference. Independent direct-quote selection and Decimal arithmetic reproduced both cost scenarios. This was the engineer-estimate false positive, not a qualifying award.

That one trade's base excess return was +0.281 percentage points against IWM and +2.486 points against the other five contractors. The IWM excess fell to +0.0043 points under stress. A negative dollar return that loses less than its benchmarks does not meet the positive-account criterion; the base IWM excess also misses the fixed +0.5-point threshold. One issuer in one calendar week cannot support the required issuer-removal robustness or a meaningful week-block confidence interval.

Jev, mini and rules produced no holdout trades. Their holdout cash accounts earned no trading profit; Jev and mini still incurred their full holdout inference costs. Earlier ORN fills remain **unknown**, because the first sufficient quote differs under the two plausible pre-November size-unit interpretations. No later quote was substituted. Consequently the full-period Jev, nano and mini account outcomes remain unknown; mini also has unresolved development inference cost. The rules account remained at $1,000 with no trades. Unknown outcomes are not zero-return investments.

All event evidence lacks verified historical publication/readiness clocks, so the computed PRIM result is a next-session **association diagnostic**, not an executable historical backtest. Intraday readiness and a Jev-specific achievable entry advantage remain unmeasured. The full reports retain skipped opportunities, missing benchmark outcomes, split/account paths, cost stress and unavailable uncertainty estimates.

The registered result for every arm is `descriptive_insufficient_sample`, with zero reviewed qualifying projects rather than the required 30 across four issuers. Independent contribution review is `inconclusive`: Jev was faster, but found no useful eligible event. The absence of positive examples also prevents claiming conventional-model equivalence. The final decision is **stop this bounded pilot without expanding the roster or starting a prospective checkpoint**. This is a stop for insufficient supporting evidence, not proof that all procurement strategies are unprofitable.

Its raw records, ledger, incomplete-run flag and downstream null financial cost were correct. Two summary counters were fixed after model execution, with a no-retry regression. Original reports and the pre-run implementation snapshot remain immutable; the accounting companion documents their interpretation.

Before any price capture, a separate execution audit corrected an unsupported size-unit assumption. [Alpaca's change notice](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/v1.1/changelog/marketdata-bid-and-ask-size-display-change) describes a switch to shares on November 3, 2025; the review could not verify whether querying older records today preserves their earlier units. Pre-switch execution therefore requires the same first sufficient quote under both raw-share and legacy-round-lot interpretations, unless units have separately verified evidence. An earlier ambiguous quote makes the fill unknown; a later convenient quote cannot replace it. Unit-invariant results retain the unit uncertainty rather than inventing displayed share counts. The correction, evidence and original implementation snapshot are archived. Three focused regressions and all 17 return tests passed before prices were opened.

The existing and integrated suite passed 469 tests once. Later source-driven fixes received focused checks, including document revisions and dates, attribution, deduplication, concurrent budget reservations, readiness, quote selection, corporate actions, IWM-based acceptance and missing outcomes. The final model-reporting regression passed with all 34 model tests. No repeated full-suite run or new model experiment was used to improve these results.

A continuation requires evidence that this pilot did not produce, and a separate opt-in; low inference cost is not a reason to expand the failed sample.
