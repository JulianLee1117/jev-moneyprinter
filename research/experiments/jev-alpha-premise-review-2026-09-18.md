# Premise review: Jev is a candidate measurement tool, not an assumed trading edge

Decision: continue one bounded test of incremental return information. Stop treating the novelty of Jev's architecture or interface as evidence that a profitable stock strategy must exist. No additional strategy families, model calls or trading infrastructure are justified by this review alone.

## What the capability establishes

TypeSafe documents parallel, isolated questions, typed outputs and full probability distributions over specified semantic categories. It says Jev is trained for calibrated decisions. These are useful properties for measuring ambiguous language. They do not introduce private information, establish stock-return calibration, or guarantee economically correct judgments. Confidence summarizes the probability distribution's shape rather than independently measuring correctness. Parallel question evaluation does not imply independent economic evidence or a coherent joint distribution over a company's future.

The published workflow evaluations compare against consensus predictions of strong external models on security, agent observability, invoices and customer service. They do not establish calibration against realized financial outcomes. TypeSafe also supplies a conventional-LLM wrapper for the same structured workflow, so probability-aware classification is not a method that became possible only with Jev. With cost savings excluded as the user's objective, the remaining claim needs evidence of superior economically relevant measurement.

## The surviving economic hypothesis

A disclosure contains a change in expected business conditions that numerical earnings information and the price reaction observed before entry do not fully capture. Better semantic measurement could help predict the remaining price adjustment. Full probability vectors might preserve useful ambiguity that hard categories discard. This is a hypothesis about incremental information, not a direct conversion of semantic confidence into return probabilities.

Historical research supports investigating unexpected language conditional on hard news and issuer-specific context. It does not establish current profitability or a Jev advantage. See [Demers and Vega](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/ifdp/2008/951/ifdp951.htm) and [Druz, Wagner and Zeckhauser](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20991). The historical settings, trading costs, long/short portfolios and timing differ from this account.

The earnings pilot used changes relative to a supplied prior release, but then collapsed eight dimensions into an untrained equal-weight score. It did not model whether the message was unexpected to investors, what had already been priced, or which dimensions predict returns after entry. For example, the recorded pre-entry adjusted-close stock reaction was +18.33% for IFF and -14.14% for TNDM; both received positive native scores. These are descriptive development observations, not permission to fit a reversal or momentum rule to this sample.

The losing pilot rejects promotion of its simple rule. Its six eligible complete-input events and two account trades cannot resolve the broader mechanism. Earlier interpretation gains likewise do not establish a native-probability trading benefit.

## Revised next study and stopping decisions

Before more inference, obtain a scalable dated source cohort, comparable numerical facts and adequate event periods. Freeze the universe, missingness policy, feature specification, feasible entry/holding rules, costs, temporal splits, model capacity and economically meaningful improvement criterion. Determine evaluation precision from development-period variance and event dependence; a convenient event count is not statistical power. If data coverage cannot support this, report a data-feasibility stop rather than collect more isolated model judgments.

Use the same information and comparable regularization for:

1. Numerical news, issuer history and already-observed price reaction alone.
2. Those controls plus conventional-model categorical features.
3. Those controls plus a competitive conventional model's full probability features.
4. Those controls plus Jev categories.
5. Those controls plus Jev full probability features.

The conventional probability arm is necessary: beating a hard-label control may only demonstrate the value of retaining uncertainty. Predeclare a competitive conventional model and model-selection budget before the return holdout, rather than selecting a weak control. Any semantic calibration checks are diagnostics on independently reviewed semantic labels; financial utility is evaluated against subsequent returns. No multiplication of correlated question probabilities into trade odds.

Keep all existing pilot observations in development. Use chronological separation, dependence-aware uncertainty estimates, and portfolio accounting for the actual $1,000 whole-share long-only account. A positive historical result requires fixed prospective paper predictions before a claim of usable alpha; current models may contain historical outcome knowledge. No live orders or background monitoring follow from this document.

- No useful incremental text signal: stop this earnings-language family.
- Text improves prediction/returns but Jev does not improve the competitive alternatives: retain the method only if it survives validation; end the Jev-specific thesis.
- Jev improves prediction but not feasible net returns: no trading edge for these constraints.
- Jev improves held-out net returns and prospective paper results: justify further validation and risk review.
- Imprecise or inconsistent evidence: record an inconclusive result. Continue only under a predeclared precision rule, not by trying families, cutoffs and horizons until one wins.

If the Jev premise fails, keep reusable source/return tools and pursue trading research selected for an economic mechanism and data advantage, with model choice optional. Do not move into another asset class merely to keep Jev central. Finding no deployable strategy is preferable to inventing evidence of one.
