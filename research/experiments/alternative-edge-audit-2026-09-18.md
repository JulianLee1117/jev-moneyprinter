# Alternative edge audit — 18 September 2026

This is a bounded, read-only comparison of SEC financing/liquidity changes and prediction-contract rule inconsistencies against the existing Tenaris experiment. No historical price outcomes were inspected, orders placed, accounts opened, or model calls charged. The accompanying `alternative-edge-census-2026-09-18.json` contains source URLs, captured metadata, file hashes and explicit sample limitations.

## Decision

Of the two alternatives, SEC issuer-specific financial-state changes offer the stronger next research direction. They provide repeatable company mapping, original documents, prior-state history and a much broader potential sample than the three TS episodes. They do **not** establish profitability or a new model-exclusive capability. The advantage to test is how many economically meaningful changes can be examined correctly per total dollar and unit of review time.

The parent research simultaneously located quantified trade-duty cash disclosures at American Woodmark and BlueLinx. Given that finding, the most efficient immediate expansion is an **issuer-first scan for direct outstanding cash claims and obligations**, followed by linkage to legal changes. That reuses the existing legal pipeline and tackles its weakest attribution step. Generic financing, liquidity and dilution changes remain a fallback family rather than another full build now. Historical cash already received is not a prospective catalyst; the outstanding balance, unresolved trigger, previous disclosure and incremental change must all be established. The two newly identified companies are seeds for mechanism development, not a claim-positive-only validation cohort.

Prediction-contract rule comparisons are worth a later bounded shadow test, but the present audit found accessible data, not an inconsistency with positive executable value. A large count of contracts is not an economic opportunity count.

## What is accessible now

Four purposively selected SEC submissions JSON files downloaded successfully without a key: PLUG, FOUR, RDW and ENVX. Counting every 2025 original 8-K in those files gives **92 filings**. A deliberately broad item filter (1.01, 2.03, 2.04 or 3.02) identifies **24 candidate filings**: 10 PLUG, 7 FOUR, 5 RDW and 2 ENVX. There are also 12 original 10-Qs and four original 10-Ks. These are complete counts within the captured recent arrays for this four-company convenience sample, not a market-wide census, independent event count, market-cap-qualified cohort, or estimate of alpha frequency. Current tickers must not be reused as historical membership without verification. [SEC submissions API](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)

SEC submissions metadata includes accession identity, form, acceptance time, filing date and primary-document name. The agency describes typical submissions API processing delay as less than a second, with longer delays possible at peaks. This says nothing about our eventual end-to-end latency. Filing date, accepted time, local receipt and earliest issuer publication remain separate fields. [SEC API update schedule](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)

Direct curl attempts at the SEC quarterly master index and one original exhibit returned HTTP 403. The original exhibit was readable through the web research tool, and `data.sec.gov` metadata worked. Therefore **reliable bulk original-document capture has not passed**. Production collection needs a truthful declared contact user agent, caching, bounded retries and fair-access handling; the SEC limit is 10 requests per second in aggregate. Do not route around a block or assume metadata access proves document access. [SEC access guidance](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-search-assistance/accessing-edgar-data)

The Kalshi public REST endpoint also worked without authentication. One Economics-category response contained **879 series templates**, which includes inactive and overlapping categories and is not a count of active events. The complete `KXFEDDECISION` open-market response contained **55 contracts**, with no remaining cursor. An order book was captured for the alphabetically first ticker, `KXFEDDECISION-26DEC-C25`, without searching for favorable quotes. ## SEC experiment: concrete mechanism and failure modes

Shift4's July 3, 2025 8-K provides a useful original-source development case. Its linked credit amendment is approximately 1.63 MB of HTML. Section 6.15 marks a covenant threshold changing from 3.00 to 3.10. The surrounding state matters: the test is conditional, cure rights exist, and term-loan consequences depend on revolving-lender action. A parser that strips deleted versus inserted text or treats a springing covenant as unconditional can create a false risk signal. This is an extraction stress case, not a finding that this filing was mispriced. [Filing index and accepted time](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794669/0001193125-25-155254-index.html), [original amendment](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794669/000119312525155254/d36834dex101.htm)

The 8-K itself already summarizes the 3.10 threshold and its 40% revolving-use trigger. A generic summary may thus suffice for the headline. The proposed Jev contribution is broader conditional-state coverage across every relevant definition, exception, subsidiary, facility and prior version, including cases a headline filter would discard. Deterministic HTML-aware redlines and section retrieval are mandatory comparators. [Original 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1794669/000119312525155254/d36834d8k.htm)

Financing metadata is direct to the issuer, but share returns remain an indirect consequence. Borrowings are not revenue; gross issuance is not net cash; authorized issuance is not completed dilution; covenant relief can accompany distress; restrictions may bind only a subsidiary or future condition. Many 8-K disclosures may arrive up to four business days after the underlying event, so checking earlier issuer releases and incorporated filings is essential. [Form 8-K instructions](https://www.sec.gov/files/form8-k.pdf)

Competition is real, but avoid the stale claim that paid PDS necessarily receives filings before the free website. SEC's 2025 update explicitly describes website-first availability. Paid dissemination still offers operational delivery services, and public access means rivals can build the same screening approach. Our comparative advantage must be measured interpretation and coverage, not assumed exclusive access. [Current SEC PDS description](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/public-dissemination-service-system-contact)

Relative to TS, this family has a broader observable source stream, clearer issuer identity and more standardized timestamps. Its disadvantage is noisy mapping from financial terms to equity value, confounding concurrent announcements, and crowded obvious financing headlines. Negative signals may also require borrow that is unavailable or costly. A positive-only paper test avoids assuming short availability but tests a different strategy and must be declared in advance.

## Prediction contracts: cleaner payout, demanding execution

The useful semantic task is to determine whether two rules imply equivalence, subset, mutual exclusion, exhaustive coverage or no provable relation. It must compare source agency, observation window, revisions, rounding, canceled events, successor entities, resolution discretion and rule updates. Jev can cheaply screen many candidate pairs and preserve uncertain pairs for deeper review. Embedding/blocking and conventional model comparisons are still needed; brute-force pair counts are not evidence of model value.

The captured Fed contract illustrates a real semantic trap: the title refers to a 25-basis-point cut, but secondary rules specify exactly one size bucket and an explicit canceled-meeting treatment. Broadly interpreting 'cut' as 'cut by at least this much' would invent a false relation. The snapshot showed a $0.01 YES bid and $0.02 YES ask, with corresponding displayed depth. This single snapshot proves data access only; it does not establish a profitable basket, concurrent executable quotes, or fills. The Kalshi book reports YES and NO bids; a YES ask is inferred from the complementary NO bid. [Market schema](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-markets), [order-book semantics](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/orderbook_responses)

The current July 7, 2026 Kalshi schedule gives general taker fees proportional to `M × 0.07 × C × P × (1-P)`, with contract-specific multipliers and rounding rules; affected maker orders have a separate formula. At multiplier 1, 100 contracts at $0.50 incur $1.75 taker fees before spread or a second leg. FEDDECISION has maker fees in the observed series metadata and schedule. An apparent one- or two-cent relation can disappear after execution costs. [Current fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)

Polymarket also exposes public discovery and books. Its current fee documentation uses category-specific taker fees and no maker fee; it cannot be treated as universally fee-free. Its rule engine includes dispute and clarification processes, and some ambiguous cases can resolve 50/50. A cross-venue match must prove identical payoffs under those cases, not merely similar titles. Venue eligibility and account access would be separate prerequisites before execution. [Fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees), [resolution](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/resolution)

If `A` logically implies `B`, buying YES on B and NO on A has a minimum unit payoff of one only if the contracts truly use binary compatible settlement semantics. Positive pre-trade value then requires full-size ask costs, fees, capital duration and adverse fill contingencies below that bound. Jev proposes the relation; code verifies payoff cases and economics. Independent orders introduce leg risk, and displayed depth can vanish. Generic within-event sums and same-contract complements should be solved arithmetically; calling these a new Jev capability would be misleading.

## Most effective bounded next experiment

1. Keep TS as a mechanism/control case. Freeze a **prospective filing cohort**, not only the issuers with known refunds: domestic operating companies in wood/building materials and metals selected from contemporaneous listing/SIC records, including firms with no claims. A first cap of 100 issuers is enough to establish access and labeling workload; expand only by a recorded selection rule. Current listings are acceptable for a prospective universe, not a retrospective survivorship-safe backtest.
2. Capture all new 10-Q/10-K and relevant 8-K source documents plus preceding versions for that cohort. Before scaling, verify original bytes, exhibit links, accepted/received timestamps, corrections and earlier issuer releases. Broad collection must preserve null observations.
3. Ask Jev parallel, evidence-bound questions across all candidate footnotes and exhibits: direct payer/beneficiary, outstanding amount versus received cash, recognized income versus cash, gross/net amount, legal case, unresolved trigger, timing, uncertainty and change from the prior disclosure. Reject standalone answer confidence as a return probability. Code performs amounts, dates and net-cash arithmetic.
4. Compare deterministic retrieval/redlines, a cheap conventional model, and dense Jev under equal total cost, including downstream review. Label missed claims in randomly sampled rejects. Score complete episodes, not correlated question counts. Jev must find more correctly attributed, previously unrecognized **unresolved** cash claims per total budget to pass.
5. Only after a meaningful cohort passes source and materiality checks, freeze decision and execution rules before inspecting price outcomes. Start paper observations with actual local first-seen times. A usable cohort needs enough distinct company-events; passages and quarterly repetitions of the same claim do not substitute for events.

This audit does not justify making SEC financing and prediction markets two additional full projects. It supports one wider issuer-first evidence experiment, with financing as the fallback and contract inconsistencies as a later small shadow study. More compute is useful when it increases independently checked coverage; it does not repair sparse events, missing first-public timing or costs that exceed the candidate edge.
