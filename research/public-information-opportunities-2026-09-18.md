# Public-information feeds: follow-up assessment

September 18, 2026. Evaluation of seven public-information research proposals. Desk research only: no private feed was purchased, no Jev benchmark or event-return study was run, and no trading profitability is established.

**Decision:** These proposals have a stronger causal basis than generic consumer-social monitoring. Official decisions can change legal rights, costs, permitted earnings, or funded business. My first trading-research experiment would be a narrow USITC/Commerce event family. My preferred paid-workflow hypothesis is decision-versus-expectation analysis for selected utility proceedings, conditional on domain expertise. Bankruptcy counterparty exposure is another credible narrow experiment. Building all seven would dilute the work before any edge is demonstrated.

The general claim that obscure documents offer unclaimed trading profit is not established. A document may be new to an aggregator while the economically relevant fact was known through a hearing, issuer announcement, earlier determination, or specialist alert. Trading needs a material surprise and enough price movement remaining after feasible acquisition, interpretation and execution.

**USITC / Commerce: first trading experiment, with a corrected premise.** The September 25 trailer vote is scheduled for 11:00, the bulletin for 11:30, and the full release approximately three hours after the vote—about 14:00, not 14:30. Nothing establishes that investors wait for the full release. The replaceable bulletin is also an archival problem: today's release may not preserve yesterday's first-public information. [Official schedule](https://www.usitc.gov/calendarpad/events/usitc_vote_notational_van_type_trailers_and_092526.htm)

The mechanism is plausible, but procedural details matter. Commerce determines dumping/subsidies and rates; ITC determines injury. Prior affirmative stages can already create deposit requirements. A final outcome can merely confirm expectations. On September 17, Commerce's tin-mill announcement distinguished a 136.52% dumping margin from a 130.17% cash-deposit rate after offsets. A classifier that maps both to the same generic tariff value loses important meaning. [Process explanation](https://www.trade.gov/antidumping-and-countervailing-duty-frequently-asked-questions), [tin-mill example](https://www.trade.gov/commerce-preliminary-antidumping-duty-investigation-tin-mill-products-china)

Precompute producer/importer/customer exposure and compare the event against the previous public state. Panjiva already offers product/company supply-chain intelligence, so mapping is a competitive activity. The attachment's ticker X is stale: U.S. Steel requested suspension and delisting after its June 2025 acquisition. Historical and current issuer maps must differ appropriately. [Panjiva](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/solutions/products/panjiva-supply-chain-intelligence), [SEC merger filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1163302/000110465925060674/tm2517314d1_8k.htm)

USITC has useful open datasets, but an investigation date or Federal Register publication date is not automatically first availability. Federal Register documents commonly appear for public inspection the preceding business day. Compare all relevant announcements and preserve source snapshots. [USITC data](https://www.usitc.gov/data/index.htm), [public-inspection explanation](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/public-inspection/about.html)

**FERC and state utility commissions: strong paid-workflow hypothesis.** The useful question is what changed from the staff recommendation, settlement, proposed decision and prior guidance: allowed ROE, equity ratio, capex recovery, disallowances, cost allocation or project conditions. Approval below expectations can be adverse; rejection of an ambitious request may be unsurprising. A focused source-backed change report could save analysts time even if it generates no trade.

FERC says subscription emails typically arrive within several hours, and its database distinguishes filed and posted dates. CPUC subscriptions explicitly omit some materials. These are connector-specific coverage and timing issues. EnerKnol and S&P already cover this market; a small entrant needs a demonstrably useful niche. [FERC timing](https://www.ferc.gov/how-to-esubscribe), [FERC date definitions](https://elibrary.ferc.gov/eLibraryhelp/General_Search.htm), [CPUC coverage](https://subscribecpuc.cpuc.ca.gov/), [EnerKnol](https://enerknol.com/get-enerknol/), [S&P rate-case research](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2026/01/record-amount-of-utility-rate-requests-in-2025-amid-affordability-concerns)

**PACER: narrow the target to public-company counterparties.** Identifying newly disclosed supplier, customer, creditor or landlord exposure in a selected bankruptcy could be useful. It requires separating allegations from findings, motions from signed orders, scheduled claims from allowed claims, and gross exposure from a new economic loss. Earlier disclosures, reserves, recoverability and legal status matter.

Broad court monitoring has direct competition: Docket Alarm markets a faster stock-trader alert; 9fin offers Chapter 11 dockets and analysis. PACER currently charges $0.10/page, generally capped at $3/document with exceptions. RECAP can reduce purchases, but Free Law Project says no PACER archive is complete; Fetch can still charge the user's PACER account. A billion archived documents are not a billion independent tradable events. [Docket Alarm](https://www.docketalarm.com/pricing), [9fin](https://9fin.com/distressed), [PACER fees](https://pacer.uscourts.gov/pacer-pricing-how-fees-work), [FLP coverage](https://free.law/startups/), [RECAP Fetch](https://wiki.free.law/c/courtlistener/help/api/rest/v4/recap)

**Procurement: useful backlog research, weaker speed thesis.** USAspending documentation describes general publication within five days and a 90-day DoD/USACE delay. SAM Contract Awards and Contract Opportunities are distinct sources. Agency announcements may precede database appearance, so this does not mean an award stayed undisclosed for 90 days. [Treasury documentation](https://www.usaspending.gov/data/about-the-data-download.pdf), [SAM Awards API](https://open.gsa.gov/api/contract-awards/), [award announcement rule](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/5.303)

Use incremental transaction obligations, including de-obligations; deduplicate modifications, parent vehicles and child orders. A ceiling is not an obligation, an obligation is not immediate revenue, and revenue is not profit. The cited 2025 Economics Letters paper reports longer-term predictability mainly among large companies; it does not establish the proposed small-cap intraday strategy. Full timing/cost methodology was not accessible for verification. [IDIQ rules](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/16.504), [paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165176525001727)

**NHTSA: attractive data experiment, weak initial equity hypothesis.** Complaints and manufacturer communications are accessible. Many proposed labels already exist as structured fields: make/model/year, component, crashes and injuries. Jev could add failure-pattern detail from narratives. The dictionary distinguishes incident, receipt and file-addition dates and warns that a complaint number can repeat across components. Today's records can also reflect revisions. [Datasets](https://www.nhtsa.gov/nhtsa-datasets-and-apis), [dictionary](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/ffdd/cmpl/CMPL.txt)

The proposed rate per 10,000 vehicles needs a real exposure denominator, including fleet size and age; complaint counts alone do not supply it. Reporting can rise after publicity. A recall's enterprise-value effect depends on remedy cost, scale, responsibility and prior knowledge. First test whether narrative features predict subsequently opened investigations better than existing fields and simple count trends, using actual availability dates. Separately test equity usefulness. The economics might work better for safety/quality monitoring, but buyer demand remains untested.

**FDA: conditional on specialist expertise.** Secondary manufacturer/supplier/competitor effects can be meaningful, but substitution, therapeutic equivalence, capacity, product ownership and regulatory status cannot be inferred reliably from a generic positive/negative score. The FDA explicitly separates warning-letter issue and posting dates. Backtesting on the former can create hindsight. [Warning-letter index](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/compliance-actions-and-activities/warning-letters)

**Cyber feeds: lowest initial equity priority.** CISA makes machine-readable KEV data available, much of it already structured. A vulnerable product does not identify all affected listed customers, realized losses or a predictable benefit for a rival security vendor. The attachment's paper studies reactions to cyber-attack news, not an after-cost KEV trading strategy. Useful security intelligence and useful equity signals are separate propositions. [Official data mirror](https://github.com/cisagov/kev-data), [cyber-event paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10796-025-10639-6)

**Where Jev fits.** Use it to triage, classify procedural status, distinguish requests from outcomes, and compare relevant passages. Preserve exact evidence separately and validate event-critical fields. Typed output does not make a legal conclusion correct. For the relatively small number of material events in a narrow niche, a stronger general model may be affordable; benchmark it alongside Jev and deterministic rules. The valuable asset is the accurate exposure-and-expectations history, not dependence on a particular model.

**Recommended next experiment: two-week feasibility screen, followed by prospective observation.** This is a suggested project, not work already performed.

1. Choose one trade-remedy product family with identifiable listed exposures. Find roughly 30–50 historical events if available, preserving complete investigations instead of selecting famous price movers. This sample screens feasibility; it cannot prove a durable small edge.
2. Without viewing later returns, label the event's procedural status, changed economic terms, earlier public expectations and point-in-time issuer exposure. Include null/no-change outcomes.
3. Audit first availability across agency notices, initial bulletins, public-inspection releases, issuer disclosures and news. If only dates survive, do not claim minute-level results. Start prospective snapshots where history is insufficient.
4. Compare Jev, a stronger model and rules on identical expert-reviewed material. Measure recall of material changes, false alerts and total operating effort; cheap but unreliable labels fail.
5. Prespecify one return horizon and realistic entry delay before scoring. Use executable prices where available, market/sector controls, spreads and other costs. Other horizons are diagnostics. Hold out later periods and entire case families; related countries and successive stages are correlated.
6. Keep frozen live paper alerts. Continue the trading thesis only if useful new information repeatedly arrives before an economically meaningful residual reaction. In parallel, evaluate whether analysts/importers/procurement teams would pay for the interpretation independently of returns.

Stop or change the thesis if almost all material facts were already public, prices move before feasible entry, public-company exposure is too sparse, or an inexpensive baseline performs equally well. A broad seven-feed platform is premature until at least one narrowly defined workflow earns its complexity through measured value.
